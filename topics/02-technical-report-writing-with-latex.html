Before we start

Why this matters

Your final report will have 30 figures, 15 tables and 40 references. In a word processor, adding one figure in chapter 2 means renumbering everything after it by hand.

LaTeX numbers figures, tables, sections, equations and citations for you and keeps every cross-reference correct. It is also what IEEE, ACM and most universities expect for technical writing.

This topic covers Lab II (text, errors, maths, packages) and Lab III (structure, references, figures, tables, citations). The playground on the slides compiles as you type.

Concept 1 of 21

What LaTeX is

LaTeX (pronounced “lay-tek” or “lah-tek”) is a document preparation system used worldwide for scientific papers, theses, books and slides. You write plain text with commands; a compiler turns it into a beautifully typeset PDF.

  • It separates content from style: change the document class or a package and the whole layout changes.
  • It numbers sections, figures, tables and equations automatically and keeps every cross-reference right.
  • It builds the table of contents and bibliography for you.
  • Journals and conferences (IEEE, ACM, Springer) publish LaTeX templates: you add only the content.
Overleaf is a free website that compiles LaTeX in the browser — nothing to install, and your whole team can edit the same report at once.

Getting started

Your first document

\documentclass{article}
\begin{document}
Hello World! % your content goes here...
\end{document}
  • Commands start with a backslash \.
  • Every document starts with \documentclass; the argument in { } says what kind: article, report, book, beamer (slides).
  • Optional arguments go in [ ]: \documentclass[12pt,twocolumn]{article}.
  • A % starts a comment: LaTeX ignores the rest of the line.

Everything before \begin{document} is the preamble (settings and packages). Everything between \begin{document} and \end{document} is the body that gets printed.

Handling errors

When LaTeX cannot understand the source it stops with an error and a line number. For example, misspelling \emph as \meph gives Undefined control sequence.

Advice on errors

  1. Don’t panic — errors happen to everyone.
  2. Fix them as soon as they appear: if what you just typed caused an error, start there.
  3. If there are several errors, fix the first one; the cause may even be on a line above it.
MessageUsual cause
Undefined control sequencemisspelt command, or a package not loaded
Missing $ inserted_, ^ or a maths command used outside $…$; or a blank line inside maths
Misplaced alignment tab character &an & in normal text: write \&
\begin{x} ended by \end{y}environments closed in the wrong order
Environment align undefined\usepackage{amsmath} missing
Reference … undefineda \label is missing or misspelt; or compile again

Mathematics: inline and displayed

  • Dollar signs mark maths in the text: $c = a - b + 1$. Always use them in pairs.
  • LaTeX handles spacing in maths itself — it ignores your spaces.
  • Big formulas go on their own line in an equation environment, which is numbered; equation* (from amsmath) is unnumbered.
  • Do not leave blank lines inside maths.

\begin{equation}

Environments change how commands behave: \sum is small with side limits in text, and large with limits above and below in a displayed equation.

Mathematics

amsmath: operators and aligned equations

\usepackage{amsmath}   % in the preamble

\begin{equation*}
\min_{x,y} (1-x)^2 + 100(y-x^2)^2
\end{equation*}

\begin{equation*}
\beta_i = \frac{\operatorname{Cov}(R_i, R_m)}{\operatorname{Var}(R_m)}
\end{equation*}

\begin{align*}
(x+1)^3 &= (x+1)(x+1)(x+1) \\
        &= (x+1)(x^2 + 2x + 1) \\
        &= x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1
\end{align*}
  • LaTeX reads adjacent letters as variables multiplied together, so min would print in italics. amsmath defines upright operators such as \min, \max, \log.
  • \operatorname{Cov} makes your own operator.
  • In align*, & marks the alignment point (before the =) and \\ starts a new line.
  • align without the star numbers every line; add \nonumber to skip one.

Structure

Environments and lists

\begin{itemize}   % bullet points
  \item Biscuits
  \item Tea
\end{itemize}

\begin{enumerate}  % numbers
  \item Biscuits
  \item Tea
    \begin{itemize}
      \item green      % lists can nest
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\begin{description}
  \item[SRS] Software Requirements Specification
\end{description}
  • \begin{name} … \end{name} creates an environment: a context that changes how its content is typeset.
  • itemize gives bullets, enumerate numbers, description bold labels.
  • Every \begin needs a matching \end, closed in reverse order.
  • You could even write $…$ as \begin{math}…\end{math}.

Concept 12 of 21

Title, abstract and sections

\documentclass{article}
\title{The Title}
\author{A. Author}
\date{\today}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Abstract goes here...
\end{abstract}
\tableofcontents

\section{Introduction}
The problem of \ldots
\section{Method}
We investigate \ldots
\subsection{Sample Preparation}
\subsection{Data Collection}
\section{Results}
\section{Conclusion}
\end{document}
  • Tell LaTeX the \title, \author and \date in the preamble; \maketitle prints them.
  • The abstract environment makes an abstract.
  • \section, \subsection, \subsubsection are numbered automatically (1, 1.1, 1.1.1).
  • \section* gives an unnumbered heading that is left out of the table of contents.
  • In the report class, \chapter sits above \section.
  • \tableofcontents needs two compiles to fill in.

Structured documents

Labels and cross-references

\usepackage{amsmath}   % for \eqref
...
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
In Section~\ref{sec:method}, we \ldots

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:euler}
e^{i\pi} + 1 = 0
\end{equation}
By \eqref{eq:euler}, we have \ldots
  • Put \label{key} right after the thing you number (section, equation, caption).
  • Refer to it with \ref{key}; \eqref{key} adds brackets for equations.
  • Never type numbers yourself: if you add a section, every reference updates.
  • Use prefixes so keys are readable: sec:, fig:, tab:, eq:.
  • Write Section~\ref{…}: the ~ stops the line breaking between the word and the number.
  • LaTeX needs two runs: the first records labels, the second prints them. Until then you see ??.

Concept 15 of 21

Figures and floats

\usepackage{graphicx}   % preamble
...
Figure~\ref{fig:model} shows \ldots

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{model}
  \caption{The iterative model.}
  \label{fig:model}
\end{figure}
  • \includegraphics (from graphicx) inserts JPEG, PNG or PDF images. Options: width=0.5\textwidth (half the text width), angle=270, scale=0.8.
  • Wrapping it in figure makes a float: LaTeX decides where it fits best.
  • Placement hints: h here, t top, b bottom, p own page; [H] (package float) forces “exactly here”.
  • \label must come after \caption, or the reference points to the section instead.
  • Tables go in a table float, usually with the caption above.

Figures, tables and code

Subfigures

Load caption and subcaption, then place several subfigure environments inside one figure. \hfill spreads them across the line.

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.3\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{graph1}
    \caption{$y=x$}
    \label{fig:y equals x}
  \end{subfigure}
  \hfill
  \begin{subfigure}[b]{0.3\textwidth}
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{graph2}
    \caption{$y=3\sin x$}
    \label{fig:three sin x}
  \end{subfigure}
  \caption{Three simple graphs}
  \label{fig:three graphs}
\end{figure}

Figures, tables and code

Tables

\begin{tabular}{|l|r|r|} \hline
Item   & Qty & Unit \$ \\ \hline
Widget & 1   & 199.99  \\
Gadget & 2   & 399.99  \\
Cable  & 3   & 19.99   \\ \hline
\end{tabular}
  • The argument gives the column alignment: left, centre, right; p{3cm} wraps text.
  • | draws vertical lines; \hline draws a horizontal line.
  • & separates columns and \\ ends a row — like align*.
  • For reports, the booktabs style (\toprule, \midrule, \bottomrule, no vertical lines) looks more professional.

Algorithms and code listings

\usepackage{algorithm}
\usepackage{algpseudocode}
...
\begin{algorithm}
\caption{Withdraw cash}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]   % [1] = number every line
\State $i \gets 10$
\If{$i \geq 5$}
    \State $i \gets i - 1$
\Else
    \If{$i \leq 3$}
        \State $i \gets i + 2$
    \EndIf
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Command names are title-cased: \State, \While, \EndWhile, \For, \Return. The algorithm float adds a caption and number.

\usepackage{listings}

\end{lstlisting}

Concept 19 of 21

Citations with BibTeX and biblatex

  1. Create a reference file with the .bib extension (Overleaf: New file → citation.bib).
  2. Find the paper, journal or book in Google Scholar.
  3. Click Cite → BibTeX to get the entry.
  4. Paste the entry into the .bib file.
  5. In the preamble: \usepackage{biblatex} and \addbibresource{citation.bib}.
  6. In the text: \cite{key}; where the list should go: \printbibliography.
  7. Compile (Overleaf runs biber and LaTeX again for you).
@article{guyot2002high,
  title={High solid content latexes},
  author={Guyot, A and Chu, F and Schneider, M and Graillat, C and McKenna, TF},
  journal={Progress in Polymer Science},
  volume={27}, number={8}, pages={1573--1615},
  year={2002}, publisher={Elsevier}
}
Only the entries you actually \cite appear in the list. Use one style consistently (IEEE is common in computing: \usepackage[style=ieee]{biblatex}).

  • Every figure and table has a numbered caption and is referred to in the text before it appears.

Worked example 3 of 3

Worked example 3

A figure appears as “Figure 1.1” in a reference but it is the only figure in the report. What went wrong?
Show the solution
The \label was placed before \caption (or outside the figure), so it captured the current section number (1.1). Move \label{fig:…} directly after \caption{…}.

Summary

  • LaTeX separates content from style and numbers everything automatically; Overleaf compiles it online.
  • Escape the special characters # $ % & _ { } ~ ^ \; fix the first error first.
  • Maths goes in $…$ inline or equation/equation*/align* on its own line (amsmath).
  • Load extra features with \usepackage in the preamble.
  • Structure with \section; refer with \label + \ref/\eqref — never type numbers.
  • Figures and tables are floats with \caption then \label; tables use & and \\.
  • Cite with BibTeX: .bib file, \addbibresource, \cite, \printbibliography.
