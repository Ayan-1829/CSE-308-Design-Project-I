Worked project

Meet the project: CampusCare

The university medical centre books appointments on a paper register at the reception desk. Students queue before classes, and doctors cannot see who is coming. Team Pulse (four students) builds CampusCare: a web app where students and staff book, cancel and get reminders for appointments; doctors see their queue and record visit notes; the receptionist manages doctors’ schedules.

Step 1 · Proposal and plan

Step 1 · Problem, objectives and related work

Problem statement

Students and staff need a way to book medical-centre appointments without queuing, because the reception desk handles about 120 walk-in requests a day and the average wait before being seen is 40 minutes (count taken by the team over one week). Today they queue in person, which clashes with classes and gives doctors no view of the day’s load.

SMART objectives

  1. By week 10, a registered user can book an appointment for the next 7 days in under 2 minutes.
  2. The system sends an SMS reminder 24 hours before every appointment.
  3. Doctors see their queue for the day and record a visit note for each patient.
  4. The receptionist can publish a doctor’s weekly schedule in under 5 minutes.

Step 3 · SRS

Step 3 · SRS: user classes and one feature

User classNeeds
Patient (student or staff)book, cancel, reschedule, view appointments
Doctorsee the day’s queue, record visit notes
Receptionistpublish and change doctor schedules
SMS gateway (external system)receives reminder messages

Scope: booking and reminders for the university medical centre. Out of scope: lab results, pharmacy stock, billing.

3.2 Book appointment — priority High

Stimulus/response: patient picks doctor and date → system shows free slots → patient picks one → system confirms with a reference number.

  • FR-2.1 The system shall show only slots that are free at the moment the list is displayed.
  • FR-2.2 The system shall not allow a patient to hold more than one future appointment with the same doctor.
  • FR-2.3 If the chosen slot is taken before confirmation, the system shall say so and offer the waiting list.
  • FR-2.4 The system shall give every confirmed appointment a unique reference such as CC-2291.

Decision: iterative model (22 of 30). The core requirements were clear after interviews, but doctors and students must react to real screens. Three increments fit the 11 weeks: (1) accounts and booking, (2) schedules and the doctor’s queue, (3) reminders and visit notes. A Figma prototype is shown to users before increment 1.

Step 5 · DFD

Step 5 · DFD Level 0

  • The Patient sends sign-up and booking data and receives confirmations.
  • The Receptionist supplies doctor schedules; the Doctor supplies visit notes.
  • The SMS gateway is an external system that only receives reminders.
  • No data stores yet: they are inside the system.

Step 5 · DFD

Step 5 · DFD Level 1

Balancing check: sign-up → 1.0 (registration details); booking → 2.0; confirmation ← 2.0; doctor schedules → 3.0; visit notes → 5.0; reminder ← 4.0. Every store has a flow in and a flow out.

Step 6 · Use cases

Step 6 · Use case diagram

  • Primary actors: Patient, Receptionist, Doctor. Secondary: SMS gateway.
  • «include»: Book, Cancel, View, Manage schedules and Record notes all need Log in.
  • «extend»: Join waiting list extends Book appointment only when the chosen slot is taken (FR-2.3).
  • Send reminder is started by time (the day before), not by a person.

Step 8 · Class diagram

Step 8 · Class diagram

  • User is abstract; Patient, Doctor and Receptionist inherit login data.
  • A Schedule is composed of Slots: delete a day’s schedule and its slots go too.
  • An Appointment occupies exactly one Slot; a Slot has at most one Appointment (FR-2.1).
  • An Appointment owns at most one VisitNote (composition): notes never exist without a visit.
  • The Receptionist only uses Schedule (dependency) when publishing it.
Check: every message in step 7 exists as an operation, e.g. Slot.reserve(), Schedule.freeSlots().

Summary

Summary

  • A design project is one system seen through many models: proposal, SRS, SDLC, DFD, use case, sequence, communication, class and UI.
  • Requirements get IDs in the SRS and are traced through every later model.
  • The SDLC choice is argued with a weighted matrix and tied to the schedule.
  • DFD levels balance; use cases group goals; sequence messages become class operations.
  • Wireframes are traced to use cases and tested with real users; failures lead to changes.
