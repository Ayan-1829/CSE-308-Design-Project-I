1 Introduction

1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the software requirements for release 1.0 of the University Student Management System (USMS). The USMS manages student registration, course enrollment, academic results and tuition fee payment. This SRS covers the whole system; it is the agreement between the Office of the Registrar and the development team, and the basis for design, implementation and acceptance testing.

1.2 Document conventions

  • “shall” marks a mandatory requirement; “should” marks a desirable one that may be deferred.
  • Every requirement has a unique ID: FR-nn functional, NFR-nn non-functional, BR-nn business rule. Use cases are UC-nn and test cases TC-nn.
  • Priority: High — release 1.0 cannot go live without it; Medium — needed in release 1.0 but a workaround exists; Low — may move to a later release.
  • The specified workload (used in NFR-01 and NFR-04) is 1,000 concurrent signed-in users, of whom 60% browse or enroll in courses, 30% view results and 10% pay fees, as during the first day of a registration window.

1.4 Product scope

Course registration at the university is done today with paper forms and spreadsheets: students queue at the registrar office, prerequisite and seat checks are done by hand, and results are posted on notice boards. The USMS moves these activities online so that students can register, view results and pay fees from any browser, and the registrar office can maintain records in one database.

In scope

  • User accounts and authentication
  • Course catalogue, enrollment and drop
  • Mark entry, result publication, GPA and CGPA
  • Tuition fee calculation and online payment
  • Student and course record administration

Out of scope (release 1.0)

  • Admission of new students
  • Class timetable generation
  • Hall / residence, library and transport
  • Staff payroll and human resources
  • Alumni services

2 Overall description

2.1 Product perspective

The USMS is a new, web-based system that replaces the paper forms and spreadsheets used today. It runs on a server in the university data centre with one central database, and exchanges data with two external systems: a payment gateway for tuition fees and an email / SMS service for notifications. Figure 1 shows the system boundary.

2.2 Product functions

  • Authenticate students and staff, and manage passwords.
  • Publish the course catalogue with sections, seats and schedules.
  • Enroll students in courses, checking prerequisites, seats, credit limits and time clashes; let them drop courses.
  • Record marks, publish results, and calculate GPA and CGPA.
  • Calculate tuition fees and accept online payment with receipts.
  • Let the registrar office maintain student records, course offerings and prerequisites, with an audit log.

2.3 User classes and characteristics

User classCharacteristicsMain tasks
Student (favoured class)About 8,000 users; basic computer skills; use the system mostly from phones during registration weeklog in, enroll and drop, view results, pay fees
Faculty memberAbout 300 users; occasional use, mostly at the end of a semesterenter marks for their sections
Administrator (registrar office)About 15 trained staff; daily usemaintain courses and student records, publish results
System administrator (IT)2–3 technical staffaccounts, backups, configuration, monitoring

2.4 Operating environment

  • Server: Linux server in the university data centre, a PostgreSQL 16 database and an HTTPS web server.
  • Clients: the two latest versions of Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari, on computers and on phones with screens at least 360 px wide.
  • The system must coexist with the university’s email server and the payment gateway’s public API.

2.5 Design and implementation constraints

  • The system shall be a web application; no installation on the client may be required.
  • It shall use the university’s existing PostgreSQL database server.
  • Only authorised users may access restricted academic records (University ICT and Data Protection Policy).
  • The database shall maintain data integrity: every enrollment refers to an existing student and course section.
  • The user interface shall be available in Bangla and English.

2.6 User documentation

  • Online help page, reachable from every screen.
  • A two-page PDF quick guide for students (registration and payment).
  • An administrator guide and a one-day training session for registrar office staff.

2.7 Assumptions and dependencies

  • The payment gateway provides a REST API that confirms each payment with a server-to-server callback.
  • The email / SMS service is available for notifications.
  • The registrar office will supply the course catalogue and existing student records as spreadsheets for a one-time import.
  • Every student and staff member has a university email address.

3 Specific requirements

3.1 External interface requirements

3.1.1 User interfaces

  • Responsive web pages that work on computers and phones; every page shows the signed-in user and a Log out button.
  • Each task starts from a dashboard; a student can reach enrollment, results and fees in one click.
  • Error messages state what went wrong and how to fix it (for example, which prerequisite is missing).

3.1.2 Hardware interfaces

No special hardware. The system runs on standard servers; users need only a device with a supported browser.

3.1.3 Software interfaces

  • PostgreSQL 16 database (the university’s existing server).
  • Payment gateway REST API, version 2: payment request, server-to-server status callback, refund.
  • Email / SMS service API for notifications.

3.1.4 Communications interfaces

  • All browser traffic over HTTPS (see NFR-02).
  • Payment callbacks received over HTTPS and verified with the gateway’s signature.
  • Email sent through the university SMTP server.

3.2 Functional requirements

3.2.1 User authentication

Description and priority. Students and staff sign in to use the system; accounts are protected against password guessing. Priority: High.

Stimulus / response sequences

  1. The user enters an ID and password → the system checks them and opens the user’s dashboard.
  2. The credentials are wrong → the system shows “Incorrect ID or password” and counts the failed attempt.
  3. The user chooses “Forgot password” → the system sends a reset link to the university email address.

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-01The system shall authenticate students using their student ID and password.High
FR-05The system shall authenticate faculty and staff using their staff ID and password.High
FR-06The system shall lock an account for 15 minutes after 5 consecutive failed login attempts and notify the user by email.High
FR-07The system shall let a user reset a forgotten password through a one-time link sent to the registered university email address and valid for 30 minutes.Medium

3.2.2 Course catalogue and enrollment

Description and priority. Students see the courses offered in the current semester and enroll in them during the registration window. Priority: High.

Stimulus / response sequences

  1. The student opens “Register” → the system lists the sections open for enrollment.
  2. The student selects a section → the system checks prerequisites, seats, credit limit and time clashes.
  3. All checks pass → the system enrolls the student and shows the new total credits.
  4. A check fails → the system rejects the request and explains which rule failed.

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-02The system shall display courses available for enrollment, with course code, title, credits, section, schedule and seats left.High
FR-03The system shall prevent enrollment when prerequisites are not satisfied (specified in detail below).High
FR-08The system shall reject enrollment in a section with no seats left and offer to place the student on the section’s waiting list.Medium
FR-09The system shall reject an enrollment that would take the student above the credit limit in BR-01.High
FR-10The system shall reject an enrollment whose class times clash with a course the student is already enrolled in.High
FR-11The system shall let a student drop a course before the drop deadline set by the registrar office.Medium
FR-12The system shall show the student’s current enrollment with the total number of credits.High

Detailed specification of FR-03

Requirement IDFR-03
NameCourse prerequisite validation
DescriptionThe system shall verify that a student has completed the required prerequisite courses before accepting an enrollment.
PreconditionThe student is logged in and has selected a course section during the registration window (BR-04).
InputStudent ID and the selected course ID.
Processing
  1. Retrieve the course’s prerequisite requirements.
  2. Retrieve the student’s academic record.
  3. Compare the completed courses with the required prerequisites; a prerequisite counts as completed when it was passed with grade D or higher (BR-02).
  4. Accept or reject the enrollment request.
Expected outputPrerequisites satisfied: the enrollment is accepted, subject to the other enrollment rules (FR-08 to FR-10).
Not satisfied: the enrollment is rejected and the system displays a message naming each missing prerequisite, e.g. “CSE 201 must be passed before CSE 202.”
Priority / sourceHigh · Academic Rules and Regulations; interview with the registrar office
Acceptance testTC-05. Attempt enrollment with one student who has completed the prerequisite and one who has not, all other enrollment conditions being met. The first is accepted; the second is rejected with the message naming the missing course.

3.2.3 Results and GPA

Description and priority. Faculty members enter marks, the registrar office publishes results, and students view their grades and GPA. Priority: High.

Stimulus / response sequences

  1. A faculty member opens a section → the system lists its students; the faculty member enters marks and submits.
  2. The registrar office publishes the semester’s results → students are notified by email.
  3. A student opens “Results” → the system shows grades, the semester GPA and the CGPA.

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-13The system shall let a faculty member enter and submit marks for the sections they teach, until the grade-submission deadline.High
FR-14The system shall convert marks to letter grades and grade points using the scale in BR-05.High
FR-15The system shall let an administrator publish the results of a semester; results become visible to students only after publication (BR-03).High
FR-04The system shall allow students to view published results.High
FR-16The system shall calculate the semester GPA as Σ(grade point × credits) ÷ Σ credits, and the CGPA over all completed semesters, rounded to two decimal places.High
FR-17The system shall let a student download a grade report as a PDF file.Low

3.2.4 Tuition fee payment

Description and priority. Students see what they owe for the semester and pay online through the payment gateway. Priority: Medium.

Stimulus / response sequences

  1. The student opens “Fees” → the system shows the amount due, itemised.
  2. The student chooses “Pay” → the system sends the payment request to the gateway.
  3. The gateway confirms success → the system records the payment and issues a receipt.
  4. The gateway reports failure or does not answer → the fee stays unpaid and the student can retry.

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-18The system shall calculate the tuition fee due as enrolled credits × per-credit fee + the fixed semester fee.High
FR-19The system shall let a student pay the fee through the payment gateway by card or mobile banking.Medium
FR-20The system shall record a payment and issue an electronic receipt with a unique receipt number only after the gateway confirms success.High
FR-21If the gateway reports a failure or does not respond within 60 seconds, the system shall not mark the fee as paid and shall show the student how to retry.High

3.2.5 Academic record administration

Description and priority. Registrar office staff maintain student records and course offerings. Priority: Medium.

Stimulus / response sequences

  1. An administrator searches for a student → the system shows the record; the administrator edits and saves it.
  2. An administrator opens a course → the system shows its sections, seats, schedule and prerequisites for editing.

Functional requirements

IDRequirementPriority
FR-22The system shall let an administrator create, update and deactivate student records.High
FR-23The system shall let an administrator manage course offerings: sections, seats, schedules and prerequisites.High
FR-24The system shall log every change to a student record with the user, the time, and the old and new values.Medium

3.5 Other requirements

  • Database. Student IDs and course codes shall be unique; deleting a course with enrolled students shall be refused.
  • Legal. Personal data shall be used only for academic administration, as the University ICT and Data Protection Policy requires; the audit log (FR-24) shall be kept for two years.
  • Internationalisation. All user-facing text shall be available in Bangla and English.

A Glossary

TermMeaning
Student IDThe unique number the university gives each student; used to log in.
CreditThe weight of a course; a 3-credit course meets about three hours a week.
SemesterA teaching term with its own registration window, classes and results.
SectionOne class group of a course, with its own teacher, schedule and seat limit.
PrerequisiteA course that must be completed before enrolling in another course.
EnrollmentA student’s registration in one section of a course for a semester.
Registration windowThe dates during which students may enroll in and drop courses.
Grade pointThe number (0.00–4.00) that a letter grade is worth (BR-05).
GPA / CGPACredit-weighted average of grade points for one semester / for all completed semesters.
Published resultA grade that the registrar office has released to students.
Payment gatewayAn external service that processes card and mobile-banking payments.
Specified workloadThe load defined in 1.2, used for performance and capacity tests.
