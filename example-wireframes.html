1 Introduction

This document turns the use cases into screens. The wireframes are mid-fidelity: real labels and layout, greys plus one accent colour, no final visual design. They were drawn in Figma; the clickable prototype links the screens of each flow.

1.1 Design constraints from the SRS

  • Students use phones (screens from 360 px wide) during registration week (SRS 2.3, NFR-12).
  • A student must be able to enroll in no more than five steps after logging in (NFR-07); 9 of 10 first-time testers must succeed without help (NFR-08).
  • All pages meet WCAG 2.2 AA (NFR-10) and are available in Bangla and English (SRS 2.5).
  • Error messages say what went wrong and how to fix it (SRS 3.1.1).

4 Wireframes

4.1 Enroll in a course (S-03 to S-05)

  • Seats left and “Full” are visible before tapping, so students do not try sections they cannot join.
  • The Confirm screen shows the credits after enrollment, so the credit limit is never a surprise.
  • The error names the missing course and the rule, exactly as FR-03 requires, and offers a way forward.

4.2 Pay tuition (S-08)

  • The amount is itemised (FR-18) and repeated on the Pay button, so students see exactly what they will pay.
  • The receipt number appears on screen and in the email (FR-20).

4.3 Enter marks (S-09)

  • Errors are shown next to the field, in text as well as colour (“0–100 only”).
  • Save draft lets a teacher stop and continue; Submit is the only irreversible action and is placed last.

5 Heuristic review

Heuristic (Nielsen)How the design applies it
Visibility of system statusCredits used out of 21 on Register; “saved 2 min ago” on mark entry.
Match with the real worldUniversity terms (section, credits, CGPA) and Taka amounts.
User control and freedomCancel on Confirm; Back to courses on errors; Save draft before Submit.
Consistency and standardsOne primary button per screen, always at the bottom on phones.
Error preventionSeats and “Full” shown before tapping; credits after enrollment shown before confirming; invalid marks block submission.
Recognition rather than recallCourse codes appear with titles and schedules; no codes to remember.
Help users recover from errorsError text names the missing course and offers the next step.

6 Accessibility and language

  • Text and buttons meet a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 (WCAG 2.2 AA).
  • Touch targets are at least 44 × 44 px; the small Enroll buttons sit in a full-width tappable card.
  • Errors use text and an icon, never colour alone.
  • Every field has a visible label, not just placeholder text.
  • The Bangla / English switch is on the log-in screen and in the menu of every screen; layouts allow for longer Bangla labels.

7 Usability test plan

ItemPlan
Participants10 students who have never used the USMS (NFR-08 asks for 9 of 10), and 2 faculty members for mark entry.
TasksT1 Enroll in CSE 309 Section 2. T2 Find out why CSE 202 cannot be taken. T3 Pay the semester fee in the gateway sandbox. T4 Find your CGPA. T5 (faculty) Enter and submit marks for five students.
MethodClickable Figma prototype on a phone; think aloud; the facilitator does not help.
MeasuresTask success, time on task, number of errors, steps taken, and the System Usability Scale (SUS) at the end.
Pass criteriaT1 in at most 5 steps (NFR-07); at least 9 of 10 complete T1 without help (NFR-08); SUS of at least 68.
