1 Introduction

This model describes the USMS from its users’ point of view: who the actors are, which goals the system helps them reach, and the steps of each interaction. Each use case groups related functional requirements of the SRS; the IDs UC-01 to UC-09 are those of SRS Appendix B.

2 Actors

ActorKindDescriptionGoals

StudentPrimaryAbout 8,000 students, mostly on phones during registration week.Browse, enroll, drop, view results, pay tuition
Faculty memberPrimaryAbout 300 teachers.Enter marks for their sections
AdministratorPrimaryAbout 15 registrar-office staff.Publish results, maintain records
Payment gatewaySecondary (system)External service that charges cards and mobile wallets.—
Email / SMS serviceSecondary (system)Delivers reset links, receipts and results notices.—

Actors are roles, not people: a faculty member who is also studying for a degree would use the system as two different actors.

5 Use case descriptions

The three use cases with the most rules are described in full. The format follows the topic 6 template: main success scenario first, then numbered extensions that branch from a step.

UC-03 Enroll in a course

Use caseUC-03 Enroll in a course
Primary actorStudent
Stakeholders and interestsStudent: wants the course quickly, and a clear reason if refused. Registrar office: wants the academic rules enforced. Faculty member: wants sections that stay within capacity.
PreconditionsThe student is logged in (UC-01). The registration window is open (BR-04).
TriggerThe student selects a section in the course list (UC-02).
Main success scenario
  1. The student selects a section.
  2. The system shows the section’s schedule, credits and the student’s total credits after enrollment.
  3. The student confirms.
  4. The system checks the prerequisites (include Check prerequisites).
  5. The system checks that a seat is free, that the credit limit is not exceeded (BR-01) and that there is no time clash.
  6. The system records the enrollment and shows the new total credits.
Extensions
  • 4a. A prerequisite is missing: the system names each missing course (“CSE 201 must be passed before CSE 202.”). The use case ends.
  • 5a. The section is full: the system offers the waiting list (extension point section full → Join waiting list).
  • 5b. The credit limit would be exceeded: the system shows the limit and the current total. The student may drop a course (UC-04) or choose another section.
  • 5c. The times clash with an enrolled course: the system names that course and returns to step 1.
  • *a. The registration window closes before step 6: the system refuses the enrollment; only an administrator can change it (BR-04).
PostconditionsSuccess: one enrollment with status ENROLLED; seats left falls by one; total credits are within the limit. On any failure, nothing is stored.
RequirementsFR-03, FR-08–FR-10, FR-12; BR-01, BR-02, BR-04; NFR-01, NFR-07
FrequencyAbout 40,000 enrollments in the first days of the registration window; up to 1,000 students at once (NFR-04).
Open issuesTBD-2: is a waiting-listed student enrolled automatically when a seat frees up?

UC-08 Pay tuition

Use caseUC-08 Pay tuition
Primary actorStudent
Secondary actorsPayment gateway, Email / SMS service
PreconditionsThe student is logged in and has an unpaid fee invoice for the semester (FR-18).
TriggerThe student opens Fees and chooses Pay.
Main success scenario
  1. The system shows the amount due, itemised: enrolled credits × per-credit fee + the semester fee.
  2. The student chooses Pay.
  3. The system sends a payment request to the gateway and redirects the student to the gateway’s page.
  4. The student pays by card or mobile banking.
  5. The gateway sends the system a signed payment status.
  6. The system verifies the signature, records the payment, marks the invoice paid and issues a receipt with a unique number.
  7. The system shows the receipt and emails a copy.
Extensions
  • 4a. The student cancels on the gateway’s page: the invoice stays unpaid; return to step 1.
  • 5a. The gateway reports a failure, or no status arrives within 60 seconds: the invoice stays unpaid and the system shows how to retry (FR-21).
  • 5b. A success arrives after the 60 seconds: the payment is still recorded once and the receipt emailed.
  • 6a. The signature is invalid: nothing is recorded and the event is logged for the IT administrator.
PostconditionsSuccess: exactly one payment recorded for the invoice and a receipt issued (FR-20). Always: an invoice is never marked paid without a confirmed, verified status.
RequirementsFR-18–FR-21; NFR-02, NFR-06
Open issuesTBD-1: may students pay in instalments?

UC-05 Enter marks

Use caseUC-05 Enter marks
Primary actorFaculty member
PreconditionsThe faculty member is logged in, teaches the section, and the grade-submission deadline has not passed.
Main success scenario
  1. The faculty member opens one of their sections.
  2. The system lists the enrolled students.
  3. The faculty member enters marks (0–100).
  4. The system shows each letter grade immediately, using the scale in BR-05.
  5. The faculty member saves a draft (optional; may repeat steps 3–5).
  6. The faculty member submits the marks.
  7. The system stores the marks and grades; they stay hidden from students until results are published (BR-03).
Extensions
  • 3a. A mark is outside 0–100: the field is flagged and submission is disabled until it is corrected.
  • 6a. Some students have no mark: the system lists them and refuses to submit.
  • *a. The deadline passes: the section becomes read-only; changes must go through the registrar office.
RequirementsFR-13, FR-14; BR-03, BR-05

7 Brief descriptions of the other use cases

Use caseBrief description
UC-01 Log inA user enters an ID and password; the system opens the dashboard for their role. After 5 failures the account locks for 15 minutes; “Forgot password” emails a one-time link valid for 30 minutes.
UC-02 Browse coursesA student lists the open sections of the current semester with code, title, credits, schedule and seats left, and can filter by course code.
UC-04 Drop a courseBefore the drop deadline a student drops an enrollment; the seat becomes free and total credits are updated.
UC-06 Publish resultsAn administrator checks that every section has submitted marks and publishes the semester’s results; students are notified by email.
UC-07 View resultsA student sees published grades, the semester GPA and the CGPA, rounded to two decimals.
UC-09 Maintain recordsAn administrator creates, edits or deactivates student records and course offerings and sets fee rates; every change is logged with the old and new values.
